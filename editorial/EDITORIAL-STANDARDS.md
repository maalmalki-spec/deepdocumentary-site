# Deep Documentary — Binding editorial standards v1

## Editorial voice
- Start with a vivid, historically defensible scene or a surprising verified fact; never fabricate dialogue, inner thoughts, or sensory details.
- Build a chronological narrative with stakes, investigation, evidence, competing interpretations, consequences and a reflective conclusion.
- No generic AI introductions, filler, repetition, clickbait, or unsupported certainty. Distinguish what is documented, inferred, disputed and unknown.
- Arabic must read like original modern literary Arabic, not literal translation. English must be independently edited and idiomatic.
- Target 1,500–2,500 meaningful words **per language**; expand only when evidence supports it. Use 10–14 well-developed sections, transitions and a compelling headline and deck.

## Research and citations
- Prefer primary sources: NASA, ESA, NOAA, Smithsonian, national archives, museums, academic journals and original historical records as appropriate.
- Every consequential factual assertion needs a traceable citation, ideally an inline footnote linked to a specific primary document or reliable secondary study.
- Cite precise page titles, publishers, dates and URLs; do not fabricate links, quotations, dates, studies or image credits.
- Flag unresolved claims for human verification. Contested theories must be labeled, not presented as settled facts.
- A language model without browsing cannot certify citations or factual accuracy. A human editor or independently verifiable retrieval system must review sources before publication.

## Images and visual storytelling
- Lead visual and at least 3–5 relevant inline visuals where available: archival photographs, original documents, maps, diagrams, timeline, data cards.
- Images must be directly relevant to the adjacent section, load successfully on desktop/mobile, and have descriptive bilingual captions and alt text.
- Record creator, source URL, license/rights status and required attribution. Do not assume government archives are universally public domain or use images without rights verification.
- Do not invent image URLs or silently replace authentic photographs with AI-generated imagery. Any reconstruction or AI-generated visual must be clearly labeled.
- If usable rights-cleared imagery cannot be verified, publish fewer visuals or hold the article rather than use a misleading image.

## Page design
- Match existing site black-and-gold cinematic identity, responsive typography, logo in header and footer, reading progress/TOC when practical.
- Arabic RTL and English LTR must work without broken links. Figures should have consistent aspect ratio and legible captions.
- Article must be real HTML under articles/<slug>.html, discoverable from homepage/category/archive with valid metadata, canonical and social preview where possible.

## Quality gates
1. Structural: 1,500+ meaningful words in each language, 10+ developed sections, complete title/deck, source list and visual plan.
2. Evidence: citations resolved and individually checked, claims reviewed, uncertainty marked.
3. Rights: every media file verified, attributed, licensed and loads.
4. Editorial: Arabic and English copyedited for storytelling and factual nuance.
5. Product: HTML responsive, logo visible, links and images checked, page linked from archive.
6. Finance: estimate API spend before runs and stop at project budget. A GitHub workflow is not itself a guaranteed hard spending cap.

**Publish only after all gates pass.** JSON drafts, regardless of length, are never publication-ready.
