# Deep Documentary — Editorial publishing standards

Applies to **all existing and future articles**, in Arabic and English.

1. **Trend research before topic selection.** Check primary-source news and independent reporting dated within the past 7–30 days; record the research date and links. Prefer topics with a lasting documentary angle. A trend is an editorial signal, never proof of a claim.
2. **Fill every existing category over time** (mystery, science/space, technology/AI, history/civilizations, tutorials). Do not repeatedly publish into a single category. Prioritize the least-populated category among equally newsworthy topics.
3. **At least five genuinely relevant, distinct, correctly captioned photos** per published article; prefer 6–10. All image files must be physically committed to `assets/images/<article-slug>/` and referenced by local relative URLs in article and homepage. Do not count logos, icons, repeated crops, or broken images.
4. **Licensing and provenance:** for every photo, record original source URL, creator/agency, exact license or public-domain basis, credit, subject, and article placement in `assets/images/<slug>/SOURCES.json`. No downloading an arbitrary search-result thumbnail or copying unlicensed photos. Keep evidence and credits.
5. **Quality gates:** inspect actual image bytes, format and dimensions; verify captions against the photographed event; verify local paths and rendered page; check Arabic/English text, mobile layout, SEO, canonical, sitemap, and attribution. Do not claim published or verified until checks pass.
6. **Narrative:** engaging hook, chronological human story and turning points, contextual analysis, clear separation of fact vs inference, direct links to primary sources, and update date for fast-changing stories.
7. **Draft-first:** If the five-photo local archive is incomplete, leave the article in editorial drafts rather than publishing it in homepage or sitemap. Report blockers transparently.
